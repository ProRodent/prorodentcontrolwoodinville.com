What a mouse call sounds like here.

A mouse call usually starts with a small sound or a small mess. Light scratching behind a cabinet, a rustle in a closet after midnight, or a scatter of dark pellets in a drawer. In Woodinville the calls bunch up when the autumn rain sets in and the nights turn cold, because mice move toward warm, dry buildings as the weather turns.

The first thing a technician does is confirm the animal. Young rats can leave droppings that look like mouse droppings, and the plan differs. House mouse droppings are roughly the size of a grain of rice. Larger droppings widen the inspection to include rats, which is covered on our rat control page.

House mice fit through a gap about a quarter inch wide, roughly the width of a pencil. That is smaller than most people expect. A garage door seal worn flat, a gap where a pipe enters the wall, or a dryer vent without a working flap are all large enough.

Where mice get in around local homes.

Many homes in the Pacific Northwest are wood-frame houses with siding, set over crawl spaces or daylight basements. That construction gives mice a long list of openings. Vents lose their screens, siding meets the foundation in uneven lines, and utility lines leave gaps where they pass through the framing.

Properties near wooded greenbelts see extra pressure, since a field mouse living in the trees next door can follow a branch or a fence to the roofline. Deer mice, which are common in the Northwest, behave a little differently from house mice and often nest in sheds, woodpiles and the corners of garages.

  • Garage door bottom seals and the corners where the door meets the slab.
  • Gaps around pipes, wires and cable entries in exterior walls.
  • Crawl space vents with torn or missing screens.
  • Dryer, bath fan and range hood vents with missing or bent flaps.
  • Soffit returns and gaps at the roof edge.
A mouse needs a gap the width of a pencil, and every home has a few.

How we remove mice.

Snap traps and tamper-resistant stations go on the routes we actually find, not scattered at random. Mice hug walls and follow the same path nightly, so a trap placed a foot off that path catches little. We set traps with the trigger end against the wall, in attics along rafters, in cabinets behind plumbing, and in garages along the base of shelving.

We do not rely on loose poison inside the living space. A mouse that eats bait can die inside a wall, where it causes odor and flies for weeks. If bait is appropriate, it goes in a locked station out of reach of children and pets, and the plan says why. For work in a specific room, see rodent trapping and removal.

Keeping them out for good.

Removing the mice already inside only resets the clock if the entry points stay open. After the traps do their work, we close the gaps with materials mice cannot chew. Steel wool alone rots in damp air, so we use stainless mesh, sheet metal and a sealant matched to each opening. Our exclusion and entry point sealing page lists the materials by location.

A new door sweep is often the cheapest fix on the whole job. Garage door seals harden and flatten over a few wet winters, and a replacement seal that meets the slab flush takes minutes to install.

After the visit.

What to watch for after treatment.

  • No fresh droppings in the same spot for at least a week.
  • No scratching at night in the same wall or ceiling area.
  • Traps staying empty on the follow-up visit.
  • No new gnaw marks on boxes or packaging.

If you still hear noise after the first week, tell us. Sometimes a second entry point turns up once the first is closed, and sometimes a nest in a hard-to-reach space needs another look. Both are normal parts of a mouse job, and the follow-up visit exists for exactly that.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets out of rooms with droppings, nests or open gaps until we have looked.
  • Leave dry droppings alone, because sweeping them raises dust you do not want to breathe.
  • Skip loose bait or poison, which complicates the inspection and can leave animals dying in walls.
  • Jot down the hours when you hear or see activity, since timing helps trace the route.

Questions about mouse control

How do I know it is mice and not rats?

Size is the clue. Mouse droppings are about the size of a grain of rice, and rat droppings are noticeably larger. Light, quick scratching points to mice. If you are unsure, describe it on the call.

Are deer mice different from house mice?

Somewhat. Deer mice live in rural and wooded settings and often nest in sheds and garages. Public health guidance treats their droppings with extra care, so do not sweep them dry.

How long does a mouse job take?

Many jobs need an inspection visit, a trap check within days and a sealing visit once catches stop. We give you a realistic number of visits after the inspection.

Will traps alone fix it?

Not usually. Traps remove the animals inside, but open gaps let new ones arrive. Sealing is what makes the result last.
