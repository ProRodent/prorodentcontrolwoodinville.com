Businesses that attract rodents.

Rodents look for food, shelter and quiet corners, and many businesses supply all three. Restaurants and cafes handle food and waste. Tasting rooms and shops stock cardboard, packaging and stored goods. Warehouses and production spaces have dock doors, pallets and long stretches of unsupervised floor.

Woodinville draws a lot of visitors. People come for Chateau Ste. Michelle Winery, Molbak's Garden + Home, Woodinville Whiskey Co and many tasting rooms, and the businesses that serve them face rodent pressure from deliveries, waste and shared walls.

What commercial districts look like to a rodent.

A row of tasting rooms in office-park style units shares walls, rooflines and utility chases. Rats and mice travel along those hidden pathways, so an issue in one unit can show up in the next. Delivery doors and dumpster enclosures are the busiest points, and gaps at the base of dock doors are common.

Garden retailers and nurseries add soil, seed, bulk feed and potted plants. Those are strong attractants, and open-air layouts make exclusion harder. We adjust the plan to fit, with a focus on storage, waste and the edges of the building.

  • Dock and delivery doors with worn seals.
  • Dumpster pads and enclosures with gaps at the base.
  • Shared walls, ceiling voids and utility chases.
  • Floor drains, mop sinks and wet areas.
  • Stored cardboard, seed and dry goods.
In a shared building, a rodent problem is never just one unit's problem.

How a commercial job is run.

We begin with an inspection of the premises, inside and out, and a conversation with the manager about operations, delivery times and past issues. The plan sets out monitoring points, treatment steps, exclusion work and the schedule of visits.

Visits are logged. We record what was found, what was done and what remains to do, and we leave a record that you can keep with your own paperwork. If an inspector or landlord asks for documentation, you have it.

Working around trading hours.

A restaurant cannot close in the middle of service, and a tasting room needs its evenings. We plan work for off hours where we can, and we use methods that fit food-handling areas, such as enclosed stations and trapping in preference to loose products. Before we arrive

  • Keep dogs and cats indoors or leashed if the activity is in the yard or garage.
  • Do not pour cleaners over droppings before the inspection.
  • Do not plug a hole yourself first, sealing it with an animal inside creates new problems.
  • Remember where you saw the animal and which direction it ran.
