Rain, cold and the calendar.

Summer is the quiet period indoors and the busy one outdoors. Rats and mice feed on fallen fruit, garden produce, pet food and compost, and burrows appear along fences and foundations. Yard habits in summer decide how many animals look for a way in later.

Why rodents show up in this area.

Redmond mixes older neighborhoods, newer subdivisions and a lot of multi-unit housing. Each has its own weak points. Older single-family streets tend to have aging crawl space vents and settled siding. Newer subdivisions have tight siding but plenty of gaps at utility penetrations. Apartment and condo buildings add shared walls and trash enclosures.

Housing age shapes where rodents get in. Older homes tend to have settled foundations, worn vents and utility penetrations that were never sealed. Newer homes have tighter siding, but gaps still appear at pipes, wires and the joints where roof meets wall. We check the building before we guess.

Garages and crawl spaces are the quiet back doors of most homes. Door seals flatten, vent screens tear, and stored boxes provide nest material. Those spaces are often unheated and rarely checked, so a problem can run for weeks before anyone notices.

Trails, creeks and green corridors.

Redmond has long green corridors, including a river trail and creek courses that run through neighborhoods. Greenways are good for residents and also act as travel routes for rats and mice. A home next to one tends to see more activity along the fence line and the back of the lot.

If your property borders a trail, a creek or a wooded buffer, mention it on the call. It changes where we put traps and which gaps we check first.
