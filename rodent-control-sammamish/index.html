Conditions that matter for the plan.

Sammamish sits on a plateau above Lake Sammamish, and much of its housing is suburban, with newer subdivisions, larger lots and wooded greenbelts between neighborhoods. Newer construction is tight, but gaps appear where pipes, wires and vents pass through framing, and landscaping beds planted tight to the foundation give rodents cover.

Trees and landscaping do a lot of the work for rodents. Branches that touch the roof become bridges, and dense plantings against the foundation give cover right at the wall. A short walk around the outside usually shows which plants and limbs matter.

Wet Pacific Northwest weather sends rodents indoors. When autumn turns cold and rainy, mice and rats look for warm, dry spaces, and a gap that went unused all summer suddenly becomes an entrance. Calls tend to rise with the first sustained rain.

Greenbelts between subdivisions.

Greenbelts and wooded buffers run between many subdivisions, and they act as travel corridors for rats and mice. A house that backs onto one tends to see activity at the fence line and in the garage.

We check the lot edge, the garage and the roofline first, then move inside. If you have a bonus room over the garage, tell us. Those spaces often have voids that rodents reach.

Many newer homes have unfinished spaces over garages or in basements. Those are quiet and rarely checked, so a problem can build there unnoticed.

How the year shapes the problem.

The first cold snap is a trigger. A sudden drop in temperature sends mice indoors within days, and garages and crawl spaces are the first places affected. If a cold night is forecast and you have not checked your seals, now is a good time.

Inside the inspection.

In the garage we check the door seal, the corners and the walls shared with the house. Stored boxes and bags of food are examined too, since they are the usual nest material and food source for mice and rats.

The walk starts at the roofline, where soffits, vents and flashing show the most common entry points for roof rats and squirrels. From there we work down the walls to the foundation, noting every gap around pipes, wires and vents.

What a visit involves.

After the inspection finds what is active and where, the plan has two main parts. Rodent trapping and removal clears the animals inside, and rodent exclusion and entry point sealing closes the gaps with metal and mesh so the problem does not return.

A return visit checks that traps stay clear and the seals hold.

For shops, restaurants and offices, commercial rodent control sets up monitoring and records, while trapping and removal clears active catches around trading hours.
