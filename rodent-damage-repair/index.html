What rodents damage.

Rodents chew constantly, because their front teeth never stop growing. That means they gnaw wood, plastic, wire insulation, pipes, drywall and anything soft enough to give. The damage ranges from cosmetic to serious, and some of it, such as chewed wiring, is a fire and safety concern.

In a wet climate, damage compounds. A chewed gap in a soffit lets rain in, damp insulation loses value, and a torn crawl space vapor barrier lets moisture rise. The sooner it is repaired, the less secondary damage there is.

  • Insulation tunneled, matted and soiled in attics.
  • Flexible ducts chewed or pulled away from fittings.
  • Soffit, fascia and trim gnawed to widen a gap.
  • Drywall corners and baseboards chewed from inside.
  • Wire insulation stripped, which is a fire risk.
  • Crawl space vapor barrier torn.
Chewed wiring is a safety issue first and a repair issue second.

Where our work stops and other trades begin.

We repair the parts that relate directly to the rodent problem, such as gaps in trim and soffit, damaged vents, torn barriers and soiled insulation. We do not do electrical work. If you see bare, chewed or damaged wiring, stay away from it and call a licensed electrician.

Likewise, rotted framing, roofing and large drywall jobs may need a general contractor. We tell you where the line falls so you can plan, and we document what we find with photos to help any trade that follows.

Repairs come after sealing.

It makes no sense to repair damage while the animals are still active. The order is inspection, trapping, sealing, then repair. After the gaps are closed, repairs hold. See rodent exclusion for how sealing works, and attic and crawl space cleanup for removing droppings and soiled material.

Insurance may treat rodent damage differently from other kinds of damage, and many policies exclude it. Check yours, keep photos and receipts, and ask us for notes on what was found.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets out of rooms with droppings, nests or open gaps until we have looked.
  • Leave dry droppings alone, because sweeping them raises dust you do not want to breathe.
  • Skip loose bait or poison, which complicates the inspection and can leave animals dying in walls.
  • Jot down the hours when you hear or see activity, since timing helps trace the route.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Do you repair electrical wiring?

No. If wiring is chewed, keep clear of it and call a licensed electrician. We document what we find.

Is rodent damage covered by insurance?

Often not, but policies vary. Keep photos and receipts and ask your insurer.

Can I do small repairs myself?

Small gaps can be patched, but repairs before sealing and removal can trap animals or be chewed through again.

How much will repairs cost?

It depends on the damage. You see a price for each item before work starts.
