What industrial units offer a rodent.

A production or storage unit has warm floors, long wall lines, stored materials and plenty of quiet corners behind equipment. Pallets of packaging, cardboard cases, bags of ingredients and bottling supplies all provide nest material. Floor drains and wash areas supply water.

Many industrial-style units use roll-up or dock doors. Any door propped open for air, customers or deliveries lets in whatever is outside, and gaps at the bottom of the door and along its tracks are the next route even when it is closed.

  • Roll-up door seals and tracks.
  • Dock doors and delivery entries.
  • Gaps around pipes, conduits and glycol or utility lines.
  • Floor drains, trench drains and wash areas.
  • Stored cardboard, pallets and packaging.
  • Dumpster areas at the back of the units.
An open door on a nice evening is also an open door for rodents.

Sealing and housekeeping.

Door seals, brush strips, closed floor-drain covers and sealed penetrations are the foundation of exclusion for an industrial unit. After that comes housekeeping. Keep cardboard off the floor, store dry goods in sealed bins, and take waste out on a regular schedule. Our rodent exclusion page covers the materials.

Tasting-room operators have an added concern, which is customer perception. A single sighting can hurt a visit, so quiet, fast responses matter. The line is answered 24/7, and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows.

A housekeeping list for production and tasting space.

Habits that deter rodents.

  • Keep cardboard and packaging off the floor and away from walls.
  • Store dry goods and ingredients in sealed bins.
  • Keep floor drains covered when not in use.
  • Remove waste on a regular schedule and keep dumpster lids closed.
  • Walk the door lines at close and look for gaps or droppings.

None of this replaces sealing, but it removes the food and cover that keep rodents interested. Pair it with monitoring and a record of what is found.

Before we arrive

  • Clear a path to the attic hatch, the garage wall and the cabinet under the kitchen sink.
  • Leave droppings undisturbed, protective cleanup is part of the visit.
  • Avoid setting snap traps where children or pets can reach them.
  • Make a note of any odor, since smell can point to where an animal has died.
