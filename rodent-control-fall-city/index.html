How the setting changes the job.

Fall City sits along the Snoqualmie River, with a small center and a good deal of rural land around it. Properties often have outbuildings, animals and open ground. The river corridor gives rats cover, and farm-style buildings bring feed and stored goods.

Drainage and edges draw rats. Ditches, creeks, retaining walls and the lines where lawn meets forest are travel routes. A property that borders one of them tends to see more activity at the fence line and along the foundation.

Wet Pacific Northwest weather sends rodents indoors. When autumn turns cold and rainy, mice and rats look for warm, dry spaces, and a gap that went unused all summer suddenly becomes an entrance. Calls tend to rise with the first sustained rain.

Common reasons for a call.

Here are the usual first signs. None of them proves a problem alone, but two together almost always do.

  • Rustling in the kitchen cabinets at night.
  • Droppings in a drawer or on a shelf.
  • Torn bags of dry goods.
  • Gnawing sounds from inside a wall.
  • Signs in a garage or basement that spread upstairs.

Where the walk-through starts.

Next we look at the crawl space or lower level if the house has one. Torn vent screens, soiled insulation and burrows along the foundation tell us where activity is and which route the animals use to reach the living space.

Inside the house we look in the kitchen, the utility room and any room where you heard or saw activity. Gaps around pipes under sinks, behind appliances and around the water heater are common routes between floors.
