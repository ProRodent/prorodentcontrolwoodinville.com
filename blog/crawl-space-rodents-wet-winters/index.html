A damp, dark crawl space is a quiet shelter. Here is how rodents use it and how to find out whether yours has a problem.

Many homes in the Pacific Northwest sit over a crawl space. It holds plumbing, wiring, ductwork and insulation, and most people go down there rarely. That makes it a favorite for rodents, who value quiet, shelter and a way into the house.

This guide explains how rodents use crawl spaces, the signs to look for, the weak points to check and what a proper fix involves. It is written for homeowners, and it explains why we ask about crawl spaces on almost every call.

Why rodents like crawl spaces.

A crawl space is dark, protected and close to the warmth of the house. Soil along the edges is easy to dig, and insulation and duct wrap provide soft material for nests. Pipes, wires and the rim of the foundation give easy paths in every direction.

In a wet climate, a crawl space often holds extra moisture. Damp attracts some insects, which in turn attract rodents, and it softens wood. Moisture also hides odors for a time, so a problem can build for months before you notice it.

Signs of rodents below the floor.

  • Scratching or scurrying under the floor, especially at night.
  • Droppings along the foundation or on top of the vapor barrier.
  • Torn or displaced vapor barrier and soiled insulation.
  • Chewed duct wrap, flexible ducts or wire coverings.
  • Burrows or holes along the outside of the foundation.
  • A musty or ammonia smell near floor vents.

Some signs show up in the house itself. A smell that rises from a floor register, cold drafts in one room or a pet that stares at the floor can all point to something under the floor.

The weak points at the foundation.

Foundation vents are the first thing to check. The screens on them tear, rust or get pushed out, and a gap of half an inch is all a rat needs. Next are the places where pipes, wires and cables pass through the foundation wall, which often have gaps around them.

Access doors are another weak point. A hatch with a worn seal or a missing latch gives a direct way in. Cracks in the foundation, gaps where the siding meets the concrete and missing soil beneath the sill plate are also common.

A proper fix, step by step.

Start with an inspection from inside and outside. We look for entry points, active runs, nests and damage. Then we trap, placing traps along the foundation and on routes inside. When catches stop, we seal the openings with stainless mesh, sheet metal and masonry-grade patching.

Cleanup comes last. Droppings, soiled insulation and torn vapor barrier may need replacement. We follow public health guidance, wetting droppings before removal and bagging waste, and we never sweep dry. See our attic and crawl space cleanup page for details.

Sealing the vents with proper mesh is often the single most useful change. A well-fitted stainless mesh over a vent keeps rodents out and still lets air through, which matters for moisture control.

Moisture is part of the problem.

A crawl space that stays wet is a bigger problem than just rodents. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or poor drainage can lead to mold and rot. We are not drainage or moisture contractors, and we tell you when the issue needs another trade, but we note what we find so you can follow up.

Do not go into a crawl space with signs of heavy activity without protection. Droppings and soiled insulation can release particles, and tight spaces make that worse. A mask, gloves and a good light are the minimum, and calling for help is better.

Questions to answer before a crawl space visit.

  • Where is the access, and how tight is it?
  • Is there a vapor barrier, and what condition is it in?
  • Do you hear noise under the floor, and when?
  • Is there any smell near the floor vents?

Insulation, ducts and what rodents do to them.

Under-floor insulation is soft and easy to tunnel. Rodents pull it down to line nests, and urine soaks into it. Flexible ducts with thin outer wrap are chewed, which leaks conditioned air into the crawl space and wastes energy.

If you notice that one room is colder than the others or that your heating works harder than it used to, a damaged duct or fallen insulation may be the cause. A look under the house can confirm it.

What a good inspection records.

A thorough crawl space inspection records the access point, the state of the vapor barrier and the vents, signs of activity, the extent of any damage and the likely entry points. Photographs of each are part of the record, so you can see what we saw.

From that record, the plan follows naturally. It lists the trapping steps, the sealing steps and any cleanup or repair, with a price for each. You decide what to do, and in what order.

Seasonal timing.

Autumn is the busiest time, as rodents look for winter shelter. A late-summer inspection of the vents and foundation gaps beats a winter emergency. If you hear noises under the floor now, do not wait for spring.

Before you call: safe steps for rodent signs

General safety practice drawn from CDC guidance on rodents. It is meant to keep you safe before any professional arrives.

  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests. That can put contaminated dust in the air you breathe.
  • Open doors or vents and let an enclosed space air out for 30 minutes or more before cleanup.
  • Gloves on first, handwashing last. Do both every time.
  • Soak the droppings and the surface in disinfectant first. Wiping comes only after the soak time.
  • Seal used paper towels and any dead rodent in a bag before disposal. Keep bare skin out of it.
  • Flu-like symptoms or shortness of breath after rodent exposure are a reason to see a doctor and mention the cleanup. Health questions belong with a physician, not a pest company.

Common questions

Can I check my crawl space myself?

You can look from the access door with a flashlight. If there are droppings or a strong smell, avoid entering without protection.

Will sealing vents hurt airflow?

Stainless mesh lets air through while keeping animals out. It is a common and effective fix.

We handle the rodent side and note any moisture issues so you can call the right trade.
