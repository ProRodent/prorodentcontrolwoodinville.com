What mixed housing means for a rodent plan.

When housing varies widely, so do the entry points. An older house might have a crawl space with failing vents. A newer home may have an attached garage and tight siding, but gaps at utility penetrations. A property with a large shed or a workshop adds its own risk.

The first walk around a property usually tells us more than any area description. We check the roofline, the foundation edge, the vents and the places where utilities enter, and then work indoors. If your neighbors have had a similar problem, mention it, since rodents often move along a street.

Property type.Where we look first.
Older wood-frame house.Crawl space vents, siding at the foundation, soffits.

House with a shed or workshop.Outbuilding floor, door base and stored goods.
Townhome or duplex.Shared walls, attic voids and pipe chases.

Streets near roads and open ground.

Properties near busy roads, drainage ditches or open ground often have more pressure from outdoor rodents. Ditches carry water and food debris, and open ground allows burrows. Fences, hedges and piles of stored material along the property edge give rodents protected routes.

Rodents follow edges: fences, ditches, hedges and foundations.

A simple plan for any Wedge address.

Start by looking. Check the places where pipes, wires and vents enter the house, the base of the garage door and the vents under the house. Look for droppings, gnawing and greasy rub marks. Then call with what you found and your street name.

The standard order of work applies. An inspection finds the routes. Trapping and removal clears the animals. Exclusion closes the entries. A follow-up visit confirms the result.

Before we arrive

  • Shut pets out of the room where you noticed activity until the technician has walked it.
