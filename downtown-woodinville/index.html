That mix changes how we plan. A single-family house is one building with one owner. A condo or mixed-use building is a shared structure, and a rodent problem in one unit is rarely just that unit's problem.

How rodents move through shared buildings.

In multi-unit buildings, rodents use the voids that already exist. Gaps around pipes between floors, utility chases, shared attic or ceiling spaces and the gaps behind kitchen cabinets all connect units. A mouse that nests in one unit can travel to its neighbor without ever going outside.

This is why we ask who manages the building and whether neighbors have noticed anything. Treating one unit and ignoring the rest only pushes activity around. If the problem crosses units, the best result comes when the property manager or association is involved, and we can explain what to tell them.

  • Gaps around pipes and wires between floors and units.
  • Shared roof and attic voids in townhome rows.
  • Kitchen cabinets and appliance gaps in adjoining units.
  • Parking garages and trash rooms in multi-unit buildings.
  • Planters, beds and landscaping against walls.
In a shared building, one unit's mouse is every unit's mouse.

Restaurants, shops and waste.

Food service is the main draw in any town center. Grease, food waste, spilled ingredients and cardboard all attract rodents, and dumpster enclosures with gaps at the base let them in and out. Delivery doors left open during unloading are another common route.

Retail shops add stored stock, packaging and quiet back rooms. For businesses, see our commercial rodent control page for monitoring and records. For homes and condos, mouse control and rodent exclusion cover most cases.

Condos, renters and what you can control.

Renters and condo owners often cannot seal the outside of the building. We explain what can be done from inside a unit, such as sealing gaps around pipes under sinks and behind appliances, and what needs the building manager. That keeps the conversation with them specific and short.

Keep food in sealed containers, take out the trash daily, and report droppings or noise to the manager. Written notes help, including dates and locations.

Droppings and night noises rarely clear up alone. A short call is a low-effort way to find out where you stand.

Records and who to tell.

In a building with several occupants, a written record helps. Note the date, the room and what you saw, such as droppings, noise or a live animal. Share the note with the manager or landlord, and keep a copy.

Food businesses should keep their own logs of any pest activity and the steps taken. We can describe what we found on each visit, which makes your records stronger. Ask what documentation your lease, insurer or local health authority requires.

Before we arrive

  • Clear a path to the attic hatch, the garage wall and the cabinet under the kitchen sink.
  • Leave droppings undisturbed, protective cleanup is part of the visit.
  • Avoid setting snap traps where children or pets can reach them.
