When rodent calls peak.

Autumn is the busiest season. When the first sustained rain and cold arrive, mice and rats leave wet nests in hedges and woodpiles and look for dry, warm buildings, so calls from Edmonds tend to rise in October and November. Checking vents and door seals in late summer gets ahead of that push.

Signs that prompt a call.

Any one of these is worth a call, and two together usually mean activity is current. Note when each started before you phone.

  • Thumping in the ceiling around dusk.
  • Nesting material in a vent, car engine or storage box.
  • Greasy rub marks along walls and pipes.
  • A pet suddenly fixated on one spot in the house.
  • Fresh droppings that reappear after cleaning.

Rain and falling temperatures push rodents indoors fast. If you suspect activity, call before it settles in.

Hillsides, salt air and older homes.

Near the water, hardware and flashing need to resist corrosion, so we choose stainless, copper or coated metal for sealing work. Steel that rusts within a season does not last.

On slopes, drainage and retaining walls deserve a look, since saturated soil pushes rodents toward the house.

Housing and setting.

Edmonds is a waterfront city with a historic downtown, older neighborhoods on hillsides and newer homes beyond. Older homes, sloping lots and salt air all affect which materials last. A hillside lot may put the foundation in contact with soil on one side, and older siding has had time to shift.

Wet Pacific Northwest weather sends rodents indoors. When autumn turns cold and rainy, mice and rats look for warm, dry spaces, and a gap that went unused all summer suddenly becomes an entrance. Calls tend to rise with the first sustained rain.

Drainage and edges draw rats. Ditches, creeks, retaining walls and the lines where lawn meets forest are travel routes. A property that borders one of them tends to see more activity at the fence line and along the foundation.
