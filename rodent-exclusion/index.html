Why sealing is the part that lasts.

Trapping removes the animals that are inside today. Sealing decides whether more arrive next month. Every home has openings, and rodents find them by feel, following scent trails and air currents. Close the gaps, and the problem stays solved. Leave them open, and the next generation takes the same route.

Exclusion work is quieter and slower than trapping, and it matters more. We do it after catches stop, so no animal is locked inside, and we confirm the work on a return visit.

The openings we find most often.

Wet Pacific Northwest weather is hard on building edges. Moss and moisture soften trim, siding swells and shrinks, and flashing loosens over the years. Those small failures create the gaps rodents need. A half-inch hole is plenty for a rat, and a quarter inch is plenty for a mouse.

  • Torn or missing screens on crawl space vents.
  • Gaps where pipes, wires and cables enter the exterior wall.
  • Soffit boards and fascia softened by moisture.
  • Worn garage door seals and gaps under side doors.
  • Roof-to-wall joints, chimney flashing and attic vents.
  • Gaps at foundation edges and slab cracks.
A repair is only as good as the weakest material in it.

Materials that hold up outdoors.

We choose materials for the opening and the climate. Stainless mesh and hardware cloth cover vents, because they resist rust and chewing. Sheet metal flashing closes gaps in trim and roof edges. Masonry-grade sealant fills small cracks in concrete and mortar. Copper mesh works in tight spots where a sealant bonds to it.

Steel wool and expanding foam are not enough on their own. Steel wool rusts in wet air, and rodents chew through foam in a night. They can be a layer inside a proper repair, but never the whole fix.

How the work is scheduled.

We start with the inspection, since sealing without finding every gap just leaves another one open. The plan lists the openings, the materials and the order of work. Small jobs can be done in a single visit, and larger houses with many openings take longer or need more than one trip.

After the work, a follow-up visit checks that traps are staying clear and the seals hold. If a fresh gap appears, we deal with it then. For rodents already in the attic, see attic and crawl space cleanup after the entry points are closed.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets out of rooms with droppings, nests or open gaps until we have looked.
  • Leave dry droppings alone, because sweeping them raises dust you do not want to breathe.
  • Skip loose bait or poison, which complicates the inspection and can leave animals dying in walls.
  • Jot down the hours when you hear or see activity, since timing helps trace the route.

Questions about rodent exclusion and entry point sealing

How long does sealing last?

Well-done sealing lasts for years. Stainless mesh, flashing and masonry patches do not rust or rot. The weak points are the edges and any soft material used as filler.

Can you seal while rodents are inside?

Not until catches stop. Sealing with animals inside creates new problems, so trapping comes first.

Do you replace rotted siding or trim?

We close the gap the rodents use. Full carpentry repair may need a separate trade, and we tell you where that line falls.

Will I hear the price first?

Yes. The plan and the price come after the inspection and before any work.
