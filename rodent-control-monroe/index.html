That means local permits and county public health contacts go through Snohomish County and the city. It does not change the inspection and sealing process.

The order of work.

After the inspection finds what is active and where, the plan has two main parts. Rodent trapping and removal clears the animals inside, and rodent exclusion and entry point sealing closes the gaps with metal and mesh so the problem does not return.

How the setting changes the job.

Monroe lies along the Skykomish River, with a historic center, newer developments and rural land close by. Edges of town carry farm buildings, sheds and open ground, and the river corridor offers cover and water.

Yards matter as much as houses. Bird feeders, compost, chicken feed, fruit that drops and woodpiles stacked against a wall all feed and shelter rodents. Changing a few of those habits often cuts activity before any trap is set.

Trees and landscaping do a lot of the work for rodents. Branches that touch the roof become bridges, and dense plantings against the foundation give cover right at the wall. A short walk around the outside usually shows which plants and limbs matter.

Droppings and night noises rarely clear up alone. A short call is a low-effort way to find out where you stand.

Seasons and rodent activity.

Summer is the quiet period indoors and the busy one outdoors. Rats and mice feed on fallen fruit, garden produce, pet food and compost, and burrows appear along fences and foundations. Yard habits in summer decide how many animals look for a way in later.

Town edges and open ground.

Properties at the edge of town often have outbuildings, animal feed and brush piles. Those are classic rodent attractants, and they usually come with a larger lot to inspect.

In the denser parts of town, older commercial buildings and homes close together behave more like a small city block, with shared alleys and waste areas.

The first visit in detail.

We photograph what we find, from droppings to damaged vents, so you can see the same things we did. The photos also help if you need to share the findings with a landlord, an insurer or another tradesperson.

In the garage we check the door seal, the corners and the walls shared with the house. Stored boxes and bags of food are examined too, since they are the usual nest material and food source for mice and rats.
