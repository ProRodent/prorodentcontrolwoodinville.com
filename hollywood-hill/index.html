What Hollywood Hill is, and what it is not.

Hollywood Hill is a well-known Woodinville-address community that extends beyond the incorporated city. Local real estate sources describe it as including acreage, equestrian properties, newer neighborhoods and rural-style roads. That matters for rodent control, because a property on the hill may sit outside city limits, possibly on a septic system or private well instead of city services, so ask about both when you call.

It also shares a name with the Hollywood District, the wine-country cluster of tasting rooms that wine sources place around the historic Hollywood Farm and Hollywood Schoolhouse. The district sits on the valley floor, while Hollywood Hill is the upland above it. Both are covered, but the plan differs: valley-floor properties lean toward commercial and tasting-room buildings, while upland lots lean toward trees, outbuildings and acreage. How rodents use a wooded hillside.

A hillside lot backed by forest gives rodents continuous cover from the trees to the house. Deer mice live at the forest edge and move into sheds, woodpiles and garages as the weather turns. Roof rats and squirrels follow branches across to the roof, and Norway rats follow drainage lines and low ground along the base of slopes.

Slopes also change how water moves. Runoff and drainage ditches create damp corridors that rats like, and retaining walls and daylight basements place the foundation in contact with soil on one side. We look at where the ground meets the house, since that contact point is where many entries begin.

  • Branches within a few feet of the roof or chimney.
  • Woodpiles and brush piles stacked against sheds or the house.
  • Feed bins, hay and grain in barns and outbuildings.
  • Retaining walls and drainage swales near the foundation.
  • Daylight basements and garages built into the slope.
On a wooded lot, the house is only the last stop on a long route.

Barns, sheds and animal feed.

Local sources describe equestrian properties on the hill, and any property with horses or hobby animals brings feed. A bin of grain, a bag of chicken feed or a stack of hay is an invitation. Rodents also like the tack room, where blankets and leather provide both nesting material and quiet.

We inspect outbuildings in the same way as the house. Raised floors, metal feed bins and hardware cloth over vents make a big difference. For yard and outbuilding work, see backyard and outdoor rodent control. For the house itself, rodent exclusion closes the entry points.

Septic systems and crawl spaces.

Homes outside city sewer service may have septic systems, and many houses on slopes have crawl spaces. A damaged vent or pipe penetration into the crawl space gives rodents a protected entrance, and the damp, dark space under the floor suits them. We check the vents, the vapor barrier and the penetrations, and note anything that needs repair by another trade.

If a nest or droppings have built up in the crawl space or attic, cleanup comes after removal and sealing. Public health guidance says not to sweep dry droppings, which matters even more in an enclosed space.

Rodent, squirrel or something larger.

On a wooded lot, not every noise overhead is a mouse or a rat. Squirrels are active in daylight, and rats and mice are mostly active after dark. Heavier thumping at dusk can be a rat, a squirrel or a larger animal, and the right plan differs for each.

Tell us the time of day, the kind of sound and whether you have seen the animal. Those details help us decide what to look for before we arrive, and we tell you plainly if the animal is outside what we handle.

Longer driveways and a plan for the visit.

Rural-style roads can mean long driveways, so visits need a bit of planning. Give the street name and any gate or access details on the call. If your property has a gate, a dog or livestock, tell us so the technician is prepared.

Before we arrive

  • Close the door on the busiest room if you can and keep foot traffic out of it.
  • Stay out of the attic until after the inspection, since disturbed insulation spreads dust.
  • Do not prod nests or burrows, that tends to scatter the animals into new areas.
  • Take phone photos of droppings, gnawing or damage so we can see what you saw.
