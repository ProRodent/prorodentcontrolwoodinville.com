Signs you have rodents.

Check your home for these. Two or more usually means an active problem.

  • Droppings.

    Dark pellets along baseboards, in drawers, under sinks, on garage shelves or in the attic.

  • Gnaw marks.

    Fresh gnawing leaves pale, rough edges on door frames, wiring, plastic bins and pantry boxes.

  • Noises at night.

    Scratching, rolling or scurrying in the attic, walls or ceiling once the house goes quiet.

  • Nesting material.

    Shredded insulation, paper, leaves or fabric packed into a hidden corner.

  • Rub marks.

    Greasy smears along pipes, rafters and foundation edges where animals run every day.

  • Odors.

    A musty, ammonia-like smell in a closed room, or a sharper smell that points to a dead animal.

Recognize two or more?

Rodent control for Woodinville homes and businesses

Woodinville sits in the Sammamish River valley in King County, east of Bothell, and became a city in 1993. It is best known today for wine country, but rodents do not care about that. They care about shelter, food and a dry place to spend the winter. A town with wooded hillsides, creek corridors, large lots and a lot of visitor traffic gives them all three.

The Pacific Northwest rain does much of the pushing. When autumn turns wet and cold, mice and rats look for the warmest, driest spaces nearby, which means attics, crawl spaces, garages and wall voids. Many calls arrive in the first cold snaps of the season. Others arrive in spring, when a nest started in winter finally gets loud.

What we see around 98077.

Hillside and wooded properties tend to have more cover next to the house, with trees that overhang roofs and greenbelts that run behind fences. Valley-floor properties and older streets near the town center have different weak points, such as aging crawl space vents, gaps around utility lines and worn door sweeps.

Newer construction is not immune. New homes often have unsealed gaps where pipes, wires and vents pass through framing, and landscaping beds planted tight against the foundation give rodents cover right at the wall.

Businesses and busy places.

Woodinville draws visitors to Chateau Ste. Michelle Winery, Molbak's Garden + Home and Woodinville Whiskey Co, along with many tasting rooms and restaurants. Food service, cardboard, stored goods and delivery doors are what rodents look for in any commercial area. Our commercial rodent control page covers monitoring and documentation for businesses.

For homes, the usual starting points are mouse control and rat control, with rodent exclusion closing the gaps once the animals are cleared.

Questions about rodent control in Woodinville

How can I tell whether I have mice or rats?

Droppings are the clearest clue. Mouse droppings are about the size of a grain of rice, while rat droppings are larger. Light scratching in walls often points to mice, and heavier thumping overhead or burrows near the foundation often point to rats.
